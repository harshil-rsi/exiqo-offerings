One CODE, as we understand it

  1. One portal where customers submit RFQs. Until then, RFQs are picked up from engineers' Outlook inboxes.
  2. AI reads the RFQ, pulls the data, sizes, and drafts the proposal.
  3. Application engineers review it with an accuracy score and give the final approval.
  4. Everything is observable, and results feed back into Microsoft Fabric.
  5. Later, the portal connects to Dynamics 365 and CRM to create leads and opportunities.

What the system is made of

Five parts. We will take them one at a time.

1 · Intake. RFQs arrive from engineers' inboxes now and from the portal later. Every file is stored once.

2 · Always-on agents. One files every document. One chases every open question. A heartbeat wakes them, and one workflow per RFQ holds the state.

3 · Stage agents. One specialist per stage. A person approves before the next stage starts.

4 · One gateway. Agents reach Epicor, Dynamics 365, CRM and sizing tools through one door, signed in as the engineer, inside written rules.

5 · Records. Past proposals become a searchable library. Every decision is logged with its evidence and fed back to Fabric.

Together. One RFQ moves left to right, people approve at each gate, and every number can be traced to its source.

Six clicks: overview, then intake, always-on agents, stage agents, gateway, records, then everything together. The design runs on open frameworks, so it can sit on Azure next to Fabric or on AWS.

What has worked for us

>80%

Prompt caching

Cache hit rate on agents we have built. Those tokens cost 10 to 15% of the model's list price.

<10%

Shadow models, then smaller models

A large model runs in shadow to train and grade a smaller one. A step moves over only after evaluations and A/B tests show the same quality, and then costs under a tenth of what it did.

<$200

The right model for each step

Simple steps go to small models from day one. On our PEO build that kept a month of dry runs under $200 of model spend.

The promise here is a method, not a number: accuracy first, then cost comes down as traces and evaluations show which steps are safe to make cheaper.
